Part C – Transformation of the Explanatory Variable

The severe right-skewness and curved relationship observed in the baseline data suggest that an ordinary linear model will not fit well on the original scale. We introduce a natural logarithmic transformation: \(X^{(0)} = \log_e(X) = \log(\text{gdpPercap})\).

a) Theoretical Justification for the Transformation

A logarithmic transformation is highly effective for a positive, right-skewed variable like GDP per capita for two main reasons: 1. Compresses Extreme Right-Tail Outliers: It pulls extreme, high-income observations closer to the center of the distribution while pulling out the packed lower-income values, stabilizing variance. 2. Linearizes Diminishing Return Trends: It converts an exponential or power relationship into a linear form. In economic terms, it transforms raw absolute changes into equal proportional/percentage changes, matching the realistic “diminishing returns” pattern of wealth on health.

  • Shape Comparison (Histograms): The original distribution of gdpPercap was severely right-skewed with most values crushed below $10,000. Under the log() transformation, the distribution opens up completely, shifting into a clean, nearly symmetric, bell-shaped (unimodal) distribution.
  • Scatterplot Comparison: The original scale plot showed a sharp, steep curve that flattened abruptly. The new scatterplot against log(gdpPercap) demonstrates a remarkably straight, linear trend. The red LOESS smoother line tracks almost perfectly as a straight diagonal vector from the bottom-left to the top-right.

d) Formal Symmetry Assessment (Order Statistics)

Using the quartile hinge-ratio rule discussed in module M2, we calculate whether the distribution has formally achieved structural symmetry.

# Extract the summary statistics programmatically
gdp_stats <- summary(log(gapminder_2007$gdpPercap))

lower_hinge <- gdp_stats["1st Qu."]
median_val  <- gdp_stats["Median"]
upper_hinge <- gdp_stats["3rd Qu."]

# Compute the Hinge Symmetry Ratio
hinge_ratio <- (upper_hinge - median_val) / (median_val - lower_hinge)
names(hinge_ratio) <- "Hinge Symmetry Ratio"
print(hinge_ratio)
## Hinge Symmetry Ratio 
##             0.812824
  • Symmetry Interpretation: A hinge-ratio value of 1 implies pristine mathematical symmetry. Our calculated ratio tracks very close to 1, demonstrating that the logarithmic application successfully removed the severe positive skewness and centered the explanatory distribution.

e) Final Regression Suitability Assessment

The log transformation massively improves the data structure for standard linear regression modeling. Two primary graphical reasons support this conclusion: 1. Restoration of Linearity: The bivariate relationship between \(Y\) and \(\log(X)\) is now straight rather than curved, matching the core line-fitting assumption of Ordinary Least Squares (OLS). 2. Stabilization of Spread (Homoscedasticity): The vertical variation in life expectancy is now relatively consistent across the entire horizontal range of \(\log(\text{GDP})\), eliminating the extreme funneling pattern seen on the raw scale.
